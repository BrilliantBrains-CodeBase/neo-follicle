Introduction – Unethical Practices in Hair Transplant Hair transplantation can change a person’s confidence, self-image, and emotional well-being. I have seen patients walk into my clinic with years of hesitation, embarrassment, and silent frustration. I have also seen the joy on their faces when the result looks natural, balanced, and honest. But I must say this clearly. Hair transplant surgery is also an area where many patients are misled. The demand for hair restoration has increased. Social media has made results look instant. Advertisements promise “unlimited grafts,” “celebrity surgeon treatment,” “scar-free miracle results,” and “lowest cost packages.” Behind some of these attractive claims, there are practices that are not just unethical, but unsafe. As a dermatologist, senior hair transplant surgeon, and founder of Neo Follicle Hair Transplant Clinic in Bangalore, I feel responsible to speak openly about this. My intention is not to create fear. My intention is to protect you. A good hair transplant should begin with medical honesty, not marketing drama. Hair Transplant Is a Medical Procedure, Not a Beauty Package Many people think of hair transplantation as a cosmetic treatment. In reality, it is a surgical medical procedure. It involves diagnosis, donor area assessment, surgical planning, anesthesia, graft extraction, graft handling, implantation, post-operative care, and long-term follow-up. Every step matters. Your donor area is limited. Once grafts are removed, they cannot be replaced. If the donor area is overharvested or handled badly, the damage can be permanent. This is why choosing a clinic should never be based only on price, celebrity images, or social media videos. The International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery has repeatedly warned patients about black-market hair transplant clinics and the risks of unlicensed or poorly trained personnel performing major parts of surgery. Patient education and surgeon accountability are essential in this field. The Illusion of the Surgeon: The “Ghost Surgeon” Problem One of the most disturbing practices in the industry is what I call the illusion of the surgeon. A patient is shown a famous doctor’s photo. The consultation may happen with that doctor for a few minutes. The patient is told, directly or indirectly, that the same doctor will perform the entire surgery. But on the day of the procedure, something else happens. Once the patient is sedated or lying face down, the doctor disappears. The surgery is then handled by unknown staff, temporary technicians, or poorly trained hands. This is the “ghost surgeon” tactic. The patient pays for the name of a surgeon but receives treatment from someone else. This is not transparency. This is betrayal. Let me be very honest. A hair transplant is a long procedure. It can take 6 to 8 hours or sometimes longer. It is normally a team-based procedure. No ethical clinic should pretend that one surgeon alone is continuously doing every single movement for the entire duration. But there is a major difference between a properly trained medical team working under strict doctor supervision and an unqualified team secretly replacing the surgeon without the patient’s knowledge. The first is ethical teamwork. The second is deception. Graft Splitting Scams: When Numbers Are Manipulated Another unethical practice is graft count manipulation. To understand this, you must know what a graft means. A graft is a naturally occurring follicular unit. It may contain 1, 2, 3, or sometimes 4 hairs. In a good hair transplant, these natural units are preserved carefully. But some clinics manipulate this. For example, if one natural 3-hair graft is extracted, an unethical clinic may split it into three separate single-hair grafts on paper. Then they may claim they have implanted “three grafts” instead of one. This artificially inflates the graft count. Why does this matter? Because many clinics charge per graft. So, if the numbers are manipulated, the patient may end up paying more without actually receiving more true graft units. Even worse, unnecessary splitting can reduce graft quality and affect density. This is why you should always ask: What exactly do you mean by graft? Are you counting natural follicular units or individual hairs? Will I receive a proper graft count after surgery? Will the clinic explain the difference between grafts and follicles? At Neo Follicle Hair Transplant Clinic, my team and I believe patients deserve this clarity before the procedure, not after they have paid. Fabricated Before-and-After Results Before-and-after photos are powerful. They help patients understand what is possible. But they can also be misused. Some commercial clinics use stolen photos from international websites. Some use heavily edited images. Some show results without proper lighting consistency, angles, hair length matching, or timeline clarity. A patient may see a dramatic result online and believe, “This will happen to me too.” But the photo may not belong to that clinic at all. Or it may be taken under misleading conditions. Or it may show a patient with a completely different hair loss pattern, donor quality, age, and medical history. This is why I always tell patients from Marathahalli, Whitefield, Bellandur, Electronic City, Sarjapur Road, Indiranagar, Koramangala, and Jayanagar: do not judge a clinic only by Instagram results. Ask for real cases. Ask if the results are from the same clinic. Ask if the photos are unedited. Ask whether the clinic can explain the patient’s grade of baldness, graft count, timeline, and donor area condition. Ethical before-and-after documentation should educate you. It should not emotionally manipulate you. The Dangerous “Unlimited Grafts” Promise Another red flag is the promise of “unlimited grafts.” There is no such thing as unlimited donor hair. Every patient has a limited donor reserve. The back and sides of the scalp may appear dense, but medically, we must preserve that area for long-term stability. If a clinic extracts too aggressively in one sitting, you may get patchiness, thinning, visible scarring, or an unnatural donor appearance. A responsible surgeon does not ask, “How many grafts can I sell today?” A responsible surgeon asks, “How many grafts can we safely take while protecting the patient’s future?” This difference is very important. A good hair transplant is not just about today’s hairline. It is about how you will look after 5, 10, and 20 years. Why Patients Fall for These Practices I do not blame patients. Hair loss is emotional. It affects confidence. It affects social life. It affects how people feel in photographs, meetings, weddings, and professional situations. Many IT professionals visiting our Marathahalli clinic from Whitefield or Bellandur tell me they are tired of vague commercial pitches. Many patients from Electronic City, Sarjapur Road, Indiranagar, Koramangala, and Jayanagar say they have visited clinics where everything sounded too perfect, too fast, and too sales-driven. When a person is emotionally vulnerable, a low price or dramatic promise can feel tempting. But a hair transplant should never be sold like a discount package. It should be planned like a medical procedure. What True Clinical Honesty Looks Like True honesty means telling a patient when they are not a good candidate. It means saying no to an unrealistic hairline. It means not overharvesting the donor area just to create a dramatic short-term result. It means explaining that density depends on donor quality, baldness grade, hair caliber, scalp condition, and future hair loss progression. It means showing real expectations, not fantasy outcomes. It means respecting the patient’s money, trust, and body. At Neo Follicle, my goal is not to impress you with exaggerated promises. My goal is to help you make an informed decision. Sometimes that means surgery. Sometimes that means medical treatment first. Sometimes that means delaying the procedure. Sometimes that means telling a young patient that an aggressive low hairline today may become a problem later. That is not negativity. That is responsible medicine. A Simple Checklist Before Choosing a Hair Transplant Clinic Before you commit to any clinic, ask these questions: Who is the doctor responsible for my procedure? Will the doctor be present and supervising? Who will perform extraction and implantation? Are the technicians trained and permanent members of the team? How is graft count calculated? Are grafts counted as natural follicular units or individual hairs? Can I see real, verifiable before-and-after results? Will I receive a written estimate and clear cost explanation? What happens if I need follow-up care? Will the clinic protect my donor area for the future? The answers to these questions will reveal the true character of the clinic.
